Phase-wise key points

Moderate phase (1885–1905)

  • Leaders: Dadabhai Naoroji ('Grand Old Man'), Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee, G.K. Gokhale, M.G. Ranade, W.C. Bonnerjee, Badruddin Tyabji.
  • Method: '3 P' — Prayer, Petition, Protest; faith in British justice. Gokhale was Gandhi's political guru.
  • Demands: expansion of councils, simultaneous ICS exams in India, cut in military spending, separating judiciary from executive.
  • Economic critique: Naoroji's Drain Theory (Poverty and Un-British Rule in India); R.C. Dutt's Economic History of India.
  • Achievement: Indian Councils Act 1892; spread political awareness. 'Safety valve' theory says Hume founded INC to release discontent.

Extremist and Swadeshi phase (1905–1918)

  • Leaders: Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai (Lal-Bal-Pal), Aurobindo Ghosh.
  • Partition of Bengal (Curzon) → Swadeshi: boycott of foreign goods, national schools, swadeshi industries.
  • National education: Bengal National College (1906, Aurobindo principal); Bengal Chemicals (P.C. Ray). Tilak used Ganapati (1893) and Shivaji (1895) festivals.
  • Split at Surat (1907); reunion and Congress–League pact at Lucknow (1916). Home Rule Leagues (1916).
  • British response: Morley–Minto separate electorates (1909) — seed of communal politics.

Gandhian era (1917–1934)

  • Early local satyagrahas: Champaran 1917 (indigo), Ahmedabad 1918 (mill workers), Kheda 1918 (land revenue).
  • Non-Cooperation (1920–22): surrender titles, boycott courts, schools, councils, foreign cloth; withdrawn after Chauri Chaura.
  • Civil Disobedience (1930–34): break salt law at Dandi; Gandhi–Irwin Pact suspends it (1931); resumed 1932, withdrawn 1934.
  • Gap rule: NCM 1920 → CDM 1930 → QIM 1942 (roughly every 10–12 years).
  • Constructive work: khadi, Hindu–Muslim unity, removal of untouchability (Harijan Sevak Sangh 1932), basic education.

Final phase (1935–1947)

  • GOI Act 1935 → 1937 elections → Congress ministries → resignation over WWII (1939).
  • Offers: August Offer 1940 → Cripps 1942 → Wavell / Simla 1945 → Cabinet Mission 1946 → Mountbatten Plan 1947.
  • Quit India 1942: leaderless mass rising, underground work (JP, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta).
  • INA: Mohan Singh (1942) → Subhas Bose (1943); captured Andamans renamed Shaheed and Swaraj Dweep.
  • Pressure in 1945–46: INA trials, RIN mutiny, labour strikes — Britain decides to leave.

1. Who presided over the first INC session?

W.C. Bonnerjee (Bombay, 1885, 72 delegates).

2. At which session was 'Purna Swaraj' adopted and who presided?

Lahore, Dec 1929 — Jawaharlal Nehru.

3. What did the Poona Pact (1932) replace?

Separate electorates for the Depressed Classes (Communal Award) — replaced by reserved seats (148) in joint electorates.

4. Why did Gandhi withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement?

Chauri Chaura violence, 4 Feb 1922.

5. Which act introduced dyarchy in the provinces?

Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford).

6. Who was the only Congress representative at the 2nd RTC?

Mahatma Gandhi.

7. Who was the first individual satyagrahi (1940)?

Vinoba Bhave.

8. Where was the Quit India resolution passed?

Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay — 8 Aug 1942.

Turrebaz Khan (with Maulvi Allauddin).

10. Who presided over the first Andhra Mahasabha (1930)?

Suravaram Pratapa Reddy, at Jogipet.

11. Which Viceroy was in office during Jallianwala Bagh?

Lord Chelmsford.

C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru (1 Jan 1923).

Bengal — the Renaissance

ReformerSociety / organisationWorks and key points
Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772–1833)
Atmiya Sabha (1815); Brahmo Sabha, 20 Aug 1828 → Brahmo Samaj
'Father of the Indian Renaissance'. Campaign led to abolition of Sati (Regulation XVII, 1829, Bentinck). Hindu College (1817, with David Hare), Vedanta College (1825). Papers: Sambad Kaumudi, Mirat-ul-Akhbar. Books: Tuhfat-ul-Muwahhidin, Precepts of Jesus. Title 'Raja' from Mughal emperor Akbar II; died at Bristol (1833).
Debendranath Tagore
Tattvabodhini Sabha (1839); led Brahmo Samaj from 1843 → Adi Brahmo Samaj (1866)
Revived the Brahmo Samaj; Tattvabodhini Patrika. Father of Rabindranath Tagore.
Keshab Chandra Sen
Brahmo Samaj of India (1866)
Split from Debendranath. Native (Brahmo) Marriage Act 1872. His group split again in 1878 → Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (Ananda Mohan Bose, Sivanath Shastri) after he married his minor daughter to the Cooch Behar prince.
Henry Vivian Derozio
Young Bengal Movement; Academic Association (1828)
Teacher at Hindu College; radical rationalism; dismissed in 1831.
Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
Principal, Sanskrit College; Bethune School supporter
Campaign led to Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act 1856 (Canning). Against child marriage and polygamy. Bengali primer 'Barnaparichay'. Opened Sanskrit College to non-Brahmins.
Radhakanta Deb
Dharma Sabha (1830)
Orthodox body that opposed the abolition of Sati — 'counter-reform'.
Swami Vivekananda (Narendranath Datta)
Ramakrishna Mission, 1 May 1897 (Belur Math HQ)
Disciple of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa. Parliament of Religions, Chicago, 11 Sep 1893. Practical Vedanta and service — 'service to man is service to God'. Birthday 12 Jan = National Youth Day.

Memory tricks

  • Kavitrayam = Nannaya → Tikkana → Errana (Telugu Mahabharata, in that order).
  • Chinese trio = Fa-Hien (Chandragupta II) → Hiuen Tsang (Harsha) → I-Tsing.
  • Kanishka's court = Ashvaghosha, Charaka, Vasumitra (4th Buddhist Council) — 'ACV'.
  • Harsha's court = Banabhatta (Harshacharita) + Harsha's own 3 plays.
  • Abul Fazl writes the 'A' books — Akbarnama, Ain-i-Akbari.
  • Two Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi — Barani and Shams-i-Siraj Afif.
  • Prisons gave books — Gita Rahasya (Mandalay), Discovery of India (Ahmednagar), Agnidhara (Nizam's jail), Why I Am an Atheist (Lahore).
